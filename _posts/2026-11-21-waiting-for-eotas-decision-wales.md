Your child is already missing education, but their EOTAS request is still being considered. What should happen while you wait?

**Start here:** write to the school and local authority, describe the education your child is currently missing, and ask what will start now, who will arrange it and when it will be reviewed.

For a child of compulsory school age, section 19A of the Education Act 1996 requires a Welsh local authority to arrange suitable education where illness, exclusion or other circumstances mean a child in its area may otherwise go without it. Suitability includes age, ability, aptitude and any ALN. A pending EOTAS request needs to be considered alongside the child's present access to education. [[1]](#ref-1)

This guide focuses on the waiting period. For the wider framework, see [EOTAS explained in Wales](https://blog.learnwithoutlimitscic.org/2026/02/22/eotas-explained/).

## What happens during the wait?

Ask two questions separately:

- When and how will the EOTAS request be decided?
- What education can my child access before that decision is made?

Describe what is missing. For example: “My child has received no teaching since [date], and cannot use the uploaded work without support.” If some education is happening, explain what your child can access and what needs changing.

If you try an interim arrangement, ask how the longer-term EOTAS request will continue to be considered. Record any concerns about the temporary offer and agree when it will be reviewed.

## Who should be doing what?

Where your child remains registered at school, include the headteacher and, where relevant, the ALNCo in the discussion. Ask what the school is providing and what it has referred to the local authority.

Keeping a place available at school does not, on its own, establish that your child can access suitable education there. Ask how the proposed arrangements address the barriers you have described. [[1]](#ref-1)

There is no general rule that a child must first go without education for 15 school days. The response needs to reflect the reason for absence and the child’s circumstances.

In its section on individual disruption, Welsh Government learning continuity guidance says schools should provide education during short absences, up to 15 school days, aligned as closely as possible with peers’ learning. Where absence is likely to exceed that period, consecutively or cumulatively, it says the local authority should be ready to make arrangements. These are guidance expectations, not a universal EOTAS decision deadline. [[2]](#ref-2)

Ask for a named coordinating contact. If school and local authority staff give different answers, request a joint written explanation of their roles.

This guide concerns children of compulsory school age. For older learners under 18, Welsh Government distinguishes a power to arrange EOTAS from the duty described here. Other ALN responsibilities may apply. [[1]](#ref-1), [[3]](#ref-3)

## Does your child need an IDP first?

No. Welsh Government’s March 2026 guidance expressly states that an individual development plan is not required to receive EOTAS. [[3]](#ref-3)

If your child has an IDP, ask which additional learning provision (ALP) is being delivered during the wait. Broadly, the maintained school or local authority maintaining the plan must secure its ALP. Relevant treatment or services identified through the statutory NHS arrangements have separate NHS securing duties. Ask who is responsible for each part of the provision. [[4]](#ref-4), [[5]](#ref-5)

If the plan no longer fits your child's circumstances, request an early review. Welsh Government guidance highlights reviewing IDPs around changes to EOTAS provision. [[3]](#ref-3), [[4]](#ref-4)

An IDP review and interim education can be discussed alongside each other. Our [guide to IDP support that is not happening](https://blog.learnwithoutlimitscic.org/2026/11/20/idp-support-not-happening-wales/) helps distinguish provision missing in practice from a plan that needs changing.

## Work being sent home is only part of the picture

Check whether your child can learn through what has been offered:

| What has been offered? | What needs checking? |
| --- | --- |
| Online work | Is there a suitable device and reliable internet? Can your child understand the instructions and ask for help? |
| Live lessons | Are the timing, pace and communication demands manageable? |
| Worksheets | Who supplies printed copies if needed, explains unfamiliar material and gives feedback? |
| Tuition away from home | Can your child reach and use the setting, with appropriate support? |
| A short introductory session | What happens after it, and when will the arrangement be reviewed? |
| Any scheduled tuition | What happens if the tutor cancels or the provider is unavailable? |

Ask who will review learning, progress and whether the support is sufficient. Describe barriers precisely: “My child logged in but could not follow the instructions or ask for help.” If a session cannot be accessed, ask staff to investigate why and consider adjustments.

Explain any teaching or supervision you are being expected to provide, and limits on your availability, equipment or ability to meet costs. Ask what assistance will be arranged so the plan is workable for your household.

## When health affects what your child can manage

Welsh Government’s healthcare-needs guidance calls for collaborative planning involving education, health, parents and the learner. It also recognises that shorter periods of absence can be significant, depending on the circumstances and whether suitable education is available. [[6]](#ref-6)

Ask relevant professionals to explain what helps or hinders learning. Would shorter sessions, different timing or a quieter setting help?

Include your child’s views through conversation, messages, pictures or help from a trusted adult. Record what they communicate without supplying answers for them.

Where a small starting offer is proposed, ask what explains the hours offered. Do they reflect your child’s assessed needs and current capacity, or the provider’s availability? Those are different reasons and need different responses.

A cautious start may help an individual child. Agree what evidence will inform the review, who will consider it and how the next steps will be decided. Avoid leaving a small introductory offer in place indefinitely without reviewing whether it is sufficient.

## Ask for a short written interim plan

Ask for a short plan showing:

- teaching or learning support to be provided, including frequency and start date
- the person or service delivering it
- adjustments, equipment and any travel arrangements needed for access
- how relevant ALP will be delivered
- how absence and participation will be recorded
- a review date and a contact for missed or inaccessible sessions.

Separately, ask when the EOTAS decision is expected and what evidence remains outstanding. Clarify whether any stated timescale comes from law, guidance or a local procedure.

## If you are told to wait for funding, a tutor or the panel

Ask what remains outstanding, who is responsible for resolving it and when an update is expected. Then ask:

> “What can be arranged while that is outstanding, and who can authorise an interim arrangement?”

If a start date slips, ask for a revised date and what will happen in the meantime. Keep the discussion focused on the education gap and possible arrangements, rather than recording only that a referral remains open.

## Check attendance records as well as education delivered

Continue reporting absence through the school’s usual arrangements and explaining the reasons. An EOTAS request does not automatically authorise absence or change the attendance code. Ask which codes are being used for each part of the interim arrangement and why. Welsh Government issued revised attendance-code guidance in July 2026, including specific codes for certain agreed online provision. [[7]](#ref-7)

Our [attendance guide](https://blog.learnwithoutlimitscic.org/2026/01/attendance-guidance-for-families-in.html) explains the current distinctions in more detail.

Keep a separate record of education received. If tuition is cancelled or work cannot be accessed, tell the school and coordinating officer. Ask how the provision record and any relevant attendance entry will reflect what happened.

## An email you can adapt

> **Subject: Education arrangements while [child’s name] awaits an EOTAS decision**
>
> Dear [names],
>
> Our child’s EOTAS request is awaiting a decision. Since [date], they have received [brief factual account of education delivered].
>
> The current difficulty is [explain what is missing or inaccessible, with one example].
>
> Please confirm what education will be arranged during the decision period, who will provide it and when it will start. Please also explain how the arrangements address our child’s needs and, where applicable, the ALP in their IDP.
>
> Please identify a coordinating contact, a review date and how attendance and participation will be recorded.
>
> Separately, please confirm the stage reached by the EOTAS request, any outstanding evidence and the expected decision date.
>
> Please respond by [date], given [brief explanation of the current gap]. If you consider the existing education suitable, please explain the basis for that view, including how it is accessible to our child.
>
> Kind regards,
> [Name]

Your requested reply date is not automatically a legal deadline. Choose one proportionate to the situation and explain the urgency if your child currently has no education.

## Keep a manageable record

A brief log is enough. Record the offer, what happened, what your child could access and the response to any difficulty. Distinguish your observations from your child’s account and professional advice.

Keep important correspondence, plans and decision letters together. Record what helps too. One successful session can identify useful adjustments without establishing that your child can manage a longer day or different setting.

## If the gap continues

First, send a concise written summary to the responsible school and local authority contacts. Identify what remains missing and ask for a response from a named decision-maker.

If that does not resolve matters, distinguish the problem you need to challenge:

| The issue | Route to check |
| --- | --- |
| Delay, poor communication or failure to arrange education | The relevant school or local authority complaints procedure, and independent advice about the education duty. |
| A disputed school ALN decision or school-maintained IDP | Local authority reconsideration, where applicable. [[8]](#ref-8) |
| An appealable local authority or college ALN decision | Education Tribunal for Wales guidance and appeal requirements. [[9]](#ref-9) |

The available challenge route depends on the decision being disputed. The Tribunal does not hear every disagreement about EOTAS. Check the decision letter, the Tribunal guidance and any applicable deadlines, and seek advice about the relevant route. Do not assume that correspondence or a complaint protects an appeal time limit. [[9]](#ref-9)

For independent ALN advice, contact [SNAP Cymru through SNAP Connect or its parent enquiry form](https://www.snapcymru.org/contact/). Describe the education gap and share relevant decision letters. The service uses triage, so immediate casework is not guaranteed. [[10]](#ref-10)

Where your child is receiving little or no education, seek advice promptly alongside pursuing the complaint. Ask whether you need specialist education or public-law legal advice about a failure to arrange provision, and how urgently you need to act. Keep asking what education can start while the disagreement is addressed.
