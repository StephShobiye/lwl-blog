**Updated 20 November 2026.** Updated against Welsh Government guidance of 4 November 2026. Corrected children’s rights, college appeal routes and the meaning of the seven-week reconsideration timescale. Distinguished complaints from appeals and replaced unverified firm recommendations with official advice-finding resources.

Disagreeing about an ALN decision does not have to begin with a dispute. Ask what was decided, why and what evidence was considered. A prompt conversation can resolve misunderstandings.

If disagreement continues, identify the correct route. A problem with what an IDP says and a failure to deliver suitable provision already in it may require different action.

## Start with the decision

Ask for the decision and reasons in an accessible written form. Keep the current IDP, decision letter, relevant reports and a short chronology.

State the outcome you want, such as a clearer description of need or reconsideration of particular provision. Explain the impact on learning and participation. Current Welsh Government guidance addresses written decisions, reconsideration and appeal rights. <span id="cite-1"></span>[[1]](#ref-1)

## Who can exercise rights?

The ALN system gives rights to children, their parents and young people. It is inaccurate to say that children have no independent rights or that parents always act instead of them. Capacity, case-friend and representation arrangements may apply.

For young people beyond compulsory school age, check the young person’s consent and the basis for parental involvement. Ask for accessible support rather than assuming incapacity from a diagnosis. <span id="cite-2"></span>[[2, chs. 1 and 4]](#ref-2)

## Which route fits?

| Situation | Route to check |
|---|---|
| School decision about ALN or school-maintained IDP contents | Responsible local authority reconsideration |
| Request for the LA to maintain a school IDP | Applicable LA takeover process |
| Appealable LA or FE institution decision | Education Tribunal for Wales |
| Agreed provision not delivered or another service failure | Relevant complaints procedure and advice about other remedies |

These routes can overlap. Do not assume every missed session creates a Tribunal appeal or that a complaint replaces an available appeal. [[1]](#ref-1) [[2, chs. 26 and 32–33]](#ref-2)

## School decisions and LA reconsideration

You cannot appeal a school decision directly to the Tribunal. For the relevant school decisions, first ask the responsible LA to reconsider. Explain what you disagree with and provide the relevant correspondence.

The seven-week period described in Welsh Government guidance concerns the LA’s decision following the relevant request, with the stated exception for circumstances beyond its control. It is not a universal seven-week deadline for parents to make a request. Ask which duty and timescale apply. [[1]](#ref-1)

## Independent disagreement resolution

Local authorities must arrange independent disagreement resolution. You do not have to use it before appealing. Ask how to access it and whether it fits the issue. A representative or advocate may help you participate.

Formal disagreement resolution is different from an ordinary conversation or complaint. Check any effect on the deadline with the Tribunal or a qualified adviser. <span id="cite-3"></span>[[3, “What else can I do?” and “Time-limits”]](#ref-3)

## Tribunal appeals and deadlines

Certain LA and FE institution decisions can be appealed, including decisions about ALN and specified IDP matters. Check the decision letter and Tribunal guidance rather than assuming every part of a plan is appealable. For example, travel information in section 3D is not appealable to the Tribunal. [[2, paras. 23.13–23.14 and 23.96]](#ref-2) [[3]](#ref-3)

The ordinary appeal period is eight weeks. Formal disagreement resolution can extend it in the circumstances set out in the rules. There are other timing provisions, so establish the actual deadline for your decision promptly. Conversations or ordinary complaints do not automatically pause it. [[3, “Time-limits”]](#ref-3)

You can continue constructive discussions while protecting an appeal deadline.

## Getting help

Ask the LA about independent information, disagreement resolution and advocacy arrangements. For legal advice, use [official adviser-finding information](https://www.gov.uk/find-legal-advice) and [check legal aid eligibility](https://www.gov.uk/legal-aid/check-legal-aid). Eligibility and available help depend on the case and individual circumstances. <span id="cite-4"></span>[[4]](#ref-4) <span id="cite-5"></span>[[5]](#ref-5)

If you use Facebook, [our parent peer support community](https://www.facebook.com/groups/learnwithoutlimitscic) can help you compare experiences and prepare questions. Other families’ experiences do not establish your legal entitlement. Protect personal information and do not wait for group responses where there is urgency or a deadline.

## Keep the aim clear

The aim is appropriate support and access to education, not winning every disagreement. Be open to workable solutions while keeping the learner’s needs, wishes and rights central. Informal discussion should not become indefinite delay.
