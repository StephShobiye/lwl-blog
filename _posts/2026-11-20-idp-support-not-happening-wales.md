You worked with the school to put an Individual Development Plan in place. But something doesn’t seem to be working.

Perhaps equipment isn’t available, sessions have been missed or your child says they cannot access the help they expected.

**Raise it early. A conversation while a problem is still manageable gives everyone a better chance of resolving it before education or wellbeing deteriorates.**

**Start here:** speak to the relevant person, agree an action and check-back date, then keep a brief note. If the issue continues, identify whether delivery needs fixing or the IDP needs reviewing.

This guide focuses on school and college situations in Wales. Its practical steps are suggestions, not hurdles you must complete before seeking further help.

For an immediate safety or safeguarding concern, contact the setting’s safeguarding lead or the relevant emergency or safeguarding service. If education has stopped or is seriously disrupted, contact the setting’s leadership and the responsible local authority promptly.

## 1. Aim for a shared solution

Before the conversation, ask what your child or young person wants adults to understand and what they would like to change. They do not need to explain everything perfectly before you can raise a concern.

Go into the conversation looking for the best possible outcome for your child and a workable way forward with the school or college.

Explain your concern, listen to staff and consider different ways of organising support. Another approach may work just as well, or better, than the one you had in mind.

Focus on the outcome your child needs, rather than securing agreement with every part of your interpretation. Parents and professionals may both need to reconsider their assumptions. Ask together: “What information would help us check whether our understanding is right?”

Compromise means considering different workable ways of meeting a need. It does not mean accepting unsafe arrangements, giving up necessary support or making commitments your family cannot sustain.

> “Could we talk about how this support is working? My child is finding [situation] difficult, and I’d like us to understand what’s happening and agree a way forward.”

Families can help identify difficulties, but responsibility for organising and delivering agreed provision remains with the responsible bodies. Parents should not have to manage the system on their behalf.

## 2. Separate delivery from the contents of the plan

Read the current IDP, particularly its description of additional learning provision, or ALP. Our [IDP in Wales overview]({% post_url 2026-03-21-idp-in-wales %}) provides background.

**If appropriate provision has not happened, ask how delivery will be restored. If the provision itself needs changing, discuss whether an IDP review is required. Both issues can arise together.**

| Concern | What to establish |
|---|---|
| Agreed support appears to be missing | What was delivered, what was missed and how delivery will be restored |
| Wording is unclear | What is intended and whether the plan needs clarification |
| Support happens but isn’t helping | Whether delivery or the provision itself needs changing |
| A recommendation is absent | How it was considered and why the suggested provision was included or omitted |
| Your child cannot reach or access the setting | Which transport, accessibility or support arrangements need attention |

Broadly, the school, college or local authority maintaining an IDP must secure its ALP, with specific arrangements for provision an NHS body must secure. <span id="cite-1"></span>[[1]](#ref-1)

See also [what must be included in an IDP]({% post_url 2025-11-24-idp-series-what-must-be-included-in-idp %}).

## 3. Speak to someone who can help resolve it

For classroom arrangements, start with the teacher, tutor or additional learning needs coordinator, usually called the ALNCo. Describe the difficulty rather than assuming its cause.

> “My child says the laptop hasn’t been available in several lessons. Could we check whether there’s an equipment problem or whether they’re unsure how to access it?”

Ask what staff have noticed. If they cannot resolve it themselves, ask who can make the decision and how the concern will reach that person.

Agree what action will be taken, who will take it and when you will check whether it worked. Ask for communication in a format that works for you.

For young people beyond compulsory school age, involve them directly and check their wishes about parental involvement. The ALN system has specific consent and capacity provisions. [[1, ch. 4]](#ref-1)

## 4. Keep a brief record

You do not need weeks of evidence before asking for help. A short note can prevent everyone reconstructing the same conversation later. This example is fictional.

| Date | Concern and source | Agreed action | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Date] | Child reported difficulty accessing equipment. Staff confirmation requested. | ALNCo to check arrangements with the teacher. | Check on [date], then record whether access improved. |

Distinguish what you saw, what your child reported and what staff confirmed. Include the effect on learning or wellbeing.

> “Thanks for speaking with me. My understanding is that [name] will [action] by [date], and we’ll check back on [date]. Please let me know if I’ve misunderstood anything.”

Written notes or voice-to-text are enough. Keep relevant emails and the current IDP securely. Record what helped as well as what went wrong, particularly for future staff or setting changes.

## 5. Contact the right service

Schools and colleges cannot directly manage every NHS appointment, transport booking or social care service.

### NHS support

For a missing NHS appointment, assessment or treatment, contact the healthcare team or service administrator. Check which arrangement is involved: an NHS appointment differs from an educational programme delivered by staff following a therapist’s advice.

Where both services have responsibilities, contact them in parallel. An NHS concern does not have to be resolved before discussing its educational impact and appropriate interim support with the setting.

If discussion with healthcare staff does not help, or you do not want to approach them, contact the health board or trust’s complaints team. <span id="cite-2"></span>[[2]](#ref-2)

New concerns raised from **1 April 2026** follow **Listening to People**, including early resolution and a listening discussion. Llais provides independent complaints support. <span id="cite-3"></span>[[3]](#ref-3) <span id="cite-4"></span>[[4]](#ref-4)

Where NHS responsibilities within the ALN system are unclear, ask about the health board’s designated education clinical lead officer, or DECLO. <span id="cite-5"></span>[[5]](#ref-5)

For background, see [IDPs and individual healthcare plans]({% post_url 2026-01-02-idp-vs-ihp-in-wales %}).

### Transport

Ask who authorised and manages the arrangement. A missing vehicle needs an operational response. A refusal of assistance needs the policy, written reasons and applicable challenge route.

Local authorities have learner-travel duties for qualifying children of compulsory school age. There is no general entitlement to free post-16 transport under the Learner Travel Measure. Local policies vary, and FE learners should also ask college student services about assistance. <span id="cite-6"></span>[[6]](#ref-6) <span id="cite-7"></span>[[7]](#ref-7)

Travel arrangements can be recorded in IDP section 3D. Recording them does not itself establish transport entitlement; that section is not appealable to the Education Tribunal. [[1, paras. 23.90–23.96]](#ref-1)

### Social care

Where care and support needs affect daily living or access to education, ask the social worker or council’s social services access team about assessment or review.

There is no Wales-wide birthday when college transport automatically transfers from education to social care. Adult social care legislation applies from 18, but individual arrangements depend on assessment and applicable duties and policies. Children can also have care and support assessments. Assessment does not guarantee a particular service. <span id="cite-8"></span>[[8, ss. 3, 19 and 21]](#ref-8)

If several services are involved, ask who will coordinate their actions.

## 6. Check whether the action worked

Agree a check-back date reflecting the impact. A minor equipment issue and a problem preventing attendance need different levels of urgency.

Ask whether the action happened, whether your child can access support and whether it helped. Include their views using a suitable communication method, such as writing, pictures or a communication aid.

If the action has not happened, ask why and agree the next step. **You do not need to repeat the same informal conversation indefinitely.**

## 7. Allow support to develop with independence

As skills and confidence develop, close, consistent adult support may become scaffolding, occasional prompts or help available when needed. This can create opportunities to practise skills, make decisions and take greater control.

But less visible support is not automatically evidence of independence. Has the young person developed the skill, or are they succeeding because the existing support makes participation possible?

Challenging our own assumptions as well as those of professionals can be hard. Sometimes adults underestimate a young person. Sometimes apparent independence hides how much they are struggling.

> “Are we helping this young person become more independent, or are we either stepping back too quickly or doing things they could now learn to do themselves?”

Independence need not mean doing everything unaided. Technology, prompts and reliable access to help can support greater control.

Agree what the young person wants to try, what help will remain, early signs that the change is too difficult and how support can be restored. Needing help restored is not failure. Do not wait for attendance, learning or wellbeing to collapse.

Any reduction should reflect individual needs, developing skills and experience, rather than an assumption that reaching a particular age means less help is needed. Changes to specified IDP provision should go through the appropriate review process. [[1, ch. 25]](#ref-1)

## 8. Talk it through with other parents

If you use Facebook, our [Learn Without Limits parent peer support community](https://www.facebook.com/groups/learnwithoutlimitscic) offers a place to compare experiences and hear what helped others.

> “Has anyone resolved a similar issue? Who did you speak to, and what helped?”

Another family’s experience may suggest questions or practical approaches. It does not establish entitlement. Arrangements differ between settings and services.

Describe situations generally. Avoid uploading reports or correspondence containing personal information. Removing a name may not prevent identification through other details.

Participation is optional. Peer support complements direct communication and qualified advice. Do not wait for community responses before raising an urgent concern.

## 9. Request a review when the plan needs attention

A review may help where needs have changed, provision is unclear or insufficient, or services need a revised arrangement. It may not be needed to correct an isolated booking error or replace equipment.

You can request a review before the annual review date. The body maintaining the IDP must conduct a requested review unless it considers one unnecessary. The Code addresses notification of that decision, reasons and relevant rights and support. [[1, paras. 25.21–25.25]](#ref-1)

> “We have tried [actions], but [difficulty] continues. I’m asking for an IDP review because I think the provision needs reconsidering.”

Ask what support will be available meanwhile. If declined, ask for the decision, reasons and applicable challenge route in writing. Our [guide to a good IDP review]({% post_url 2025-11-26-idp-series-what-makes-good-idp-review %}) explains preparation.

You do not have to complete every informal step before requesting a review.

## 10. Use formal routes when needed

Formal action may be appropriate where provision repeatedly fails, responsibility remains disputed or education is being lost.

| Issue | Route to check |
|---|---|
| Delivery or service failure | Relevant setting, local authority or NHS complaints procedure |
| School-maintained IDP disagreement | Local authority reconsideration |
| Appealable local authority or college decision | Education Tribunal for Wales |
| Transport decision | Applicable transport reconsideration, appeal or complaints procedure |

A school’s decision cannot be appealed directly to the Tribunal. Not every service failure is a Tribunal matter. These routes can overlap: seek advice if unsure whether to challenge the plan, delivery or both. [[1, ch. 32]](#ref-1) [[2]](#ref-2)

See [how to challenge an ALN decision]({% post_url 2026-03-28-challenge-aln-decision-wales %}). Independent advice or advocacy can help you understand options and participate.

Seek qualified advice promptly where consequences are serious or a deadline may apply. Do not assume conversations or a complaint have paused an appeal deadline. <span id="cite-9"></span>[[9, “Time-limits”]](#ref-9)

## Early communication is part of prevention

At Learn Without Limits CIC, prevention includes helping families raise concerns while there is time to resolve them constructively.

A useful conversation brings together the young person’s experience, the family’s observations and professional knowledge. Sometimes it leads to a small change. Sometimes it shows a review or formal challenge is needed.

**Start early, stay curious and focus on whether support helps the young person access education and grow.**
