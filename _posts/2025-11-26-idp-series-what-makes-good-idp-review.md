**Updated 20 November 2026.** Removed the unsupported claim that a review under 20 minutes is automatically unlawful. Corrected NHS responsibilities and automatic LA-takeover statements, and clarified review requests, outcomes and follow-up.

A useful IDP review asks whether the plan still describes the learner’s needs accurately and whether its provision is helping them learn and participate.

It should bring together the learner’s experience, family observations and relevant professional information. The aim is a workable plan, with actions people understand.

## Before requesting a review

Raise an emerging delivery problem early. An equipment fault or isolated cancellation may be resolved through a conversation without changing the plan.

If provision is unclear, unsuitable or insufficient, needs have changed or difficulties continue, explain what a review needs to address. The Code supports ongoing engagement rather than waiting for an annual meeting to discuss progress. <span id="cite-1"></span>[[1, paras. 25.16–25.20]](#ref-1)

## When a review is requested

The school, FE institution or local authority maintaining the IDP must conduct a review requested by the child, child’s parent or young person unless it considers one unnecessary. If it decides a review is unnecessary, the Code addresses notification, reasons and information about rights and support. [[1, paras. 25.21–25.25]](#ref-1)

Where conducted following a request, the review must be completed promptly and within the applicable period: 35 school days, 35 FE term-time days or seven weeks for an LA. The Code provides an exception where circumstances beyond the body’s control make compliance impractical. Ask which period applies and when the request was received. [[1, paras. 25.22–25.23]](#ref-1)

## Prepare a short agenda

Ask the learner what they want adults to understand. Offer a communication method that suits them, such as writing, pictures, communication aids or input gathered beforehand. Physical attendance at a large meeting should not be the only way their views are heard.

Take the current IDP and a brief record of what has worked, what has not and the impact. Identify the questions you most need answered.

| Question | Useful discussion |
|---|---|
| Have needs changed? | New information and how learning is affected |
| Is provision happening? | What is delivered and what is missing |
| Is it effective? | Learning, participation and wellbeing, including the learner’s views |
| Are outcomes appropriate? | Progress and whether outcomes should remain or change |
| What happens next? | Actions, responsibility, dates and monitoring |

The review considers progress, ALN, outcomes, ALP and other relevant matters. Outcomes do not have to be rewritten simply because a review takes place. [[1, para. 25.29]](#ref-1)

## Who should contribute?

Ask the body maintaining the IDP who is coordinating the review and which professional input is relevant. An LA-maintained plan does not remove the school’s role in day-to-day delivery and evidence gathering.

NHS information requests and statutory referrals about relevant treatment or services are different processes. Do not assume every therapist must attend every meeting or that direct therapy depends on an LA delegating it. NHS securing duties arise through the ALN framework’s health arrangements. [[1, ch. 21]](#ref-1)

If NHS-secured ALP is to be changed or removed, the Code requires the review process and NHS request or agreement described in paragraph 25.32. [[1, para. 25.32]](#ref-1)

If school responsibility is disputed, ask about the applicable LA reconsideration or takeover route. Complexity, absence or EOTAS labels alone should not replace an individual decision about responsibility. [[1, chs. 18 and 26]](#ref-1)

## Independence and appropriate support

Be open to changing how support is delivered as skills develop. Scaffolding, prompts and assistive technology can support independence. Check whether the learner has acquired a skill or is succeeding because existing support makes it possible.

Agree how any change will be monitored and how help can be restored. Neither age alone nor a wish to make support less visible establishes that the learner needs less assistance.

## Judge the substance, not the stopwatch

There is no universal 20-minute minimum established by the Code. A short meeting is not automatically unlawful. Consider whether relevant information was addressed, participation was supported and decisions were explained.

If you think something important was missed, say what it was and ask how it will be considered. Keep a brief record of agreed actions and request correction of factual errors.

## After the review

Ask for the outcome and any revised IDP, who will deliver each action and what will happen meanwhile. Set a practical check-back date. A further meeting is not itself a solution to missing support.
