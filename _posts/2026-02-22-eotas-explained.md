EOTAS means Education Otherwise Than At School. In Wales, it describes education arranged by a local authority for learners who cannot attend school. It can involve tuition, a pupil referral unit or other suitable provision. It is different from a parent choosing to home educate. [[1]](#ref-1)

For families, the useful starting question is whether the child is receiving suitable education, and what arrangements are needed if they are not.

This overview explains the framework. It does not establish whether a particular child qualifies for a particular package.

## What is the legal basis for EOTAS in Wales?

Section 19A of the Education Act 1996 requires each Welsh local authority to arrange suitable education, at school or otherwise, for children in its area who:

- are of compulsory school age
- may otherwise go without suitable education for a period because of illness, exclusion or other circumstances. [[2]](#ref-2)

Suitability includes the child’s age, ability, aptitude and any additional learning needs. The duty concerns the child’s circumstances and the education available to them, rather than a diagnosis or a parent’s preference alone. [[2]](#ref-2)

There is also a separate ALN provision. Section 53 of the Additional Learning Needs and Education Tribunal (Wales) Act 2018 allows a local authority to arrange some or all of the additional learning provision (ALP) in an IDP it maintains for a child outside school. It may do so only if satisfied that it would be inappropriate for that ALP to be made in a school. [[3]](#ref-3)

These provisions address different questions. The general duty to arrange suitable education should not be confused with the conditions for arranging a child’s ALP outside school.

## Does a child need an IDP to receive EOTAS?

No. Welsh Government’s March 2026 guidance expressly says an IDP is not required for EOTAS. Where a learner has ALN, the ALN framework also applies. [[1]](#ref-1)

If there is an IDP, check who maintains it and who secures each part of the provision. Broadly, the body maintaining the plan must secure its ALP, with separate duties for relevant NHS treatment or services identified through the statutory arrangements. A change of setting may mean the plan needs reviewing. [[4]](#ref-4)

## When should families raise the question?

Raise concerns when your child is missing education or cannot use the provision offered. Explain the barriers and what has been tried. You can ask about arrangements before a difficulty becomes a prolonged gap.

For example, describe the teaching actually received, difficulties reaching or using the setting, and support that has helped or remains missing. Include your child’s views in a way they can manage.

The local authority needs to consider the individual circumstances. An attendance difficulty does not automatically establish entitlement to a chosen EOTAS package. Ask for the decision, reasons and next steps in writing.

For absence reporting, medical-evidence requests and current attendance codes, read [our updated attendance guidance for families in Wales](https://blog.learnwithoutlimitscic.org/2026/01/attendance-guidance-for-families-in.html).

## What might provision look like?

Welsh Government describes different arrangements, including individual or group tuition, community-based learning, online provision and vocational routes. Provision may be temporary or longer term. [[1]](#ref-1)

Ask how the proposed arrangement fits your child. Consider teaching, communication, timing, the learning environment and practical access. An offer on paper needs checking against what the learner can actually use.

If therapy or specialist input is proposed, ask what it is intended to achieve, who will arrange it and whether it forms part of ALP or a separate health service. EOTAS does not automatically create entitlement to every intervention a family requests.

## Is EOTAS the same as elective home education?

No. EOTAS is arranged by the local authority. Elective home education is a parental choice to take responsibility for providing suitable education. Learning at home does not, by itself, tell you which arrangement applies. [[1]](#ref-1)

If your child is at home because education has broken down, ask the school and authority to explain the position. Do not treat deregistration as an administrative shortcut to resolving missing education. Seek advice before deciding to home educate.

## What changes after compulsory school age?

The section 19A duty described above applies to children of compulsory school age. Welsh Government explains that local authorities have a power to arrange EOTAS for young people above that age but under 18. Other ALN duties may apply to older learners under their own conditions. Do not assume the same EOTAS entitlement continues to age 25. [[1]](#ref-1), [[2]](#ref-2)

## What should you ask for?

Try to leave the discussion knowing:

- what education and support will be provided, by whom and from when
- how the arrangements address the learner’s needs and any relevant IDP provision
- what transport, equipment or assistance needs arranging
- who coordinates the plan and what happens if sessions are missed
- when access, learning and progress will be reviewed.

A useful question is:

> “Please explain how these arrangements will provide suitable education for my child, how they will be accessible and who we should contact if they do not work in practice.”

If a longer-term decision is pending, ask separately what education will be provided during the wait. A panel date does not explain what a child will be taught this week.
